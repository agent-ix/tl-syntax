# tl-syntax v0.1 source-release argument

## Claim

The open claim concerns only the exact source and corpus revisions presented to
the human release owner under AP-001. It excludes crates.io publication and
downstream semantic correctness.

## Reasoning

Structural validation, strict identity/version boundaries, complete requirement
bindings, feature checks, and corpus tests address the declared deterministic
failure scenarios. Independent human review owns the final sufficiency judgment.

Issue #15 extends that identity boundary without extending semantic authority:
tl-syntax validates named bounded signals, direct Boolean proposition bindings,
and complete caller-supplied requirement context. It does not derive IR fields,
parse FRETish, interpret numeric predicates, or claim that supplied provenance is
true. Downstream producers must carry the validated bytes and their identity;
the human review boundary remains unchanged.

This argument makes no appeal to retained evidence. The `evidence/ANCHORS`
integrity boundary, the retained records it anchored, and the `tools.lock`
qualification-host identities were deleted with the collector that produced
them — the last of them under `agent-ix/tl-syntax#12`, on the preservation
constraint `agent-ix/engineering-assurance#7` released for the pre-stable phase
on 2026-09-02. Nothing was rewritten or re-sealed, and the sufficiency this
paragraph used to draw from those records is not drawn from anything else in
their place. What supports the claim now is the sealed Quoin chain over
producer bytes at the named candidate revision, and the human release owner's
own reading of the diff.

The branch-retention obligation in `agent-ix/tl-syntax#8` is closed. `tl-parse`,
`tl-rewrite`, and `tl-mltl` now pin revision `953ee82`, which is reachable from
`main`. Their own evaluator, rewrite, and release results remain a separate
program-level challenge; they no longer require retaining the old source branch.

## Sufficiency Decision

The claim remains open until the sealed Quoin chain names the candidate
revision, all blocking gaps are closed, and the human release owner records an
explicit accept or reject decision. Automated checks cannot change this status.

The close condition used to name retained evidence. It does not any more, and it
is not deferred either: what identifies the candidate is the change-assurance
record's `subject.base_revision` and every proof attestation's
`candidate_revision`, both derived from the revision the caller names and both
refused by Quoin when they disagree. That is a stricter binding than the one it
replaces, and it exists at every candidate revision rather than only at the ones
somebody collected.

## Challenges

The shared corpus is designed for downstream adoption, but those repositories'
pinning and evaluator/rewrite results are outside this crate's implementation
boundary and remain visible as a program-level challenge.

The Makefile is orchestration, not an independent trust root. The controlled
measurement in [SR-013](../reviews/SR-013-make-execution-control-measurement.md)
made an invalid Rust item fail or prevent eight of thirteen `ci` prerequisites
under the `make -k` diagnostic. A global `.IGNORE:` caused those paths to emit
ignored errors while Make treated all thirteen prerequisites as successful and
exited 0. The other five paths were unaffected by the selected compile fault;
their behaviour under `.IGNORE:` was not measured.

When a chain run reaches Quoin and produces a record, its digest bindings
constrain the content of the producer bytes actually recorded. That is not a
backstop against Make suppressing the chain's own exit status: the chain retains
nothing locally, and the `.IGNORE:` measurement produced no record while Make
still reported success. No charted or pure-gate subset survives that suppression.

The `tl-syntax-release-owner` accepts only the measured global-`.IGNORE:` result
for pre-stable development. Seventeen other execution-control spellings remain
unmeasured here, and review of local output is not a compensating control because
quiet spellings can print plausible commands without executing them. This
deviation must be re-evaluated before the first stable release candidate. It
remains an open qualification challenge under
`agent-ix/engineering-assurance#11`, not an automated pass.
